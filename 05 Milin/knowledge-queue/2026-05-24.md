## 1. #392 – Genetic testing: when it’s valuable, how to choose the right test, and what to do with the results
- source: https://peterattiamd.com/genetictesting/
- type: rss
- vault_path: 03 Resources/Biohacking
- relevance: Max is a clinic owner (Ratchatakaya, 9 branches) who likely fields patient questions about genetic testing, and as a biohacker he needs a rigorous framework to distinguish genuinely actionable genetic tests (cardiovascular, cancer, pharmacogenetics) from low-evidence functional medicine panels — directly applicable to both personal optimization and clinical decision-making for patients.
- approved: false

Genetic testing is powerful in specific contexts but widely misunderstood — its value depends entirely on asking the right clinical question first. Some findings (BRCA mutations, inherited cardiac conditions, APOE for Alzheimer's risk, pharmacogenetics) are genuinely actionable and can change screening or treatment decisions. However, most genetic data is probabilistic, not deterministic, meaning phenotype measurement (direct biomarkers) is often more clinically useful than inferring risk from DNA alone. Functional medicine genetic testing (methylation, detox pathways, supplement protocols) largely lacks clinical evidence despite biological plausibility. The framework: match the test type to a specific question, evaluate effect size and actionability, and avoid accumulating genetic data without a clear decision it would change.

## 2. A critical look at ultra-processed diets and male reproductive health
- source: https://peterattiamd.com/ultra-processed-diets-and-reproductive-health/
- type: rss
- vault_path: 03 Resources/Biohacking
- relevance: Max กำลังจะมีลูกแฝดสาวกลางปี 2026 ทำให้ reproductive health และ metabolic optimization มีความเกี่ยวข้องโดยตรง บวกกับความสนใจด้าน Biohacking—การเข้าใจว่า UPFs กระทบ hormones (FSH), sperm quality, และ cardiometabolic markers ภายใน 3 สัปดาห์ เป็น actionable insight สำหรับการ optimize diet protocol
- approved: false

Sperm concentration has dropped ~50% and total sperm count ~60% between 1973–2018, with diet—specifically ultra-processed foods (UPFs)—emerging as a key modifiable factor. A randomized crossover feeding study (n=43 men, ages 20–35) found that a 3-week UPF diet increased body fat (~1.3–1.4 kg), worsened cardiometabolic markers (LDL:HDL, blood pressure), reduced FSH by ~0.5 IU/L, and dropped total sperm motility by ~13%, even when calories were controlled. The critical unanswered question is whether UPFs cause harm through direct biological mechanisms (additives, processing byproducts, micronutrient displacement) or primarily via promoting excess caloric intake and metabolic dysfunction. The study design attempts to isolate food processing from energy intake, but methodological limitations mean 'ultra-processed' may still be a proxy label rather than a confirmed independent causal mechanism. Practical takeaway: minimizing UPFs and prioritizing whole foods likely supports both metabolic and reproductive health, regardless of calorie matching.
