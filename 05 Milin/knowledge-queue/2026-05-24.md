## 1. #392 – Genetic testing: when it’s valuable, how to choose the right test, and what to do with the results
- source: https://peterattiamd.com/genetictesting/
- type: rss
- vault_path: 03 Resources/Biohacking
- relevance: Max is a clinic owner (9 branches scaling to 20) likely offering or evaluating genetic/wellness testing for patients; as a biohacker and INTJ, this framework helps him cut through marketing noise, make evidence-based decisions about which genetic tests to integrate into clinical protocols, and avoid overselling genomic services that lack actionable utility.
- approved: false

Genetic testing is powerful but widely misunderstood — its value depends entirely on asking the right clinical question first. Some findings are deterministic and life-altering (BRCA mutations, inherited cardiac conditions, APOE for Alzheimer's risk), while most others are probabilistic and require context to interpret meaningfully. Peter Attia argues that directly measuring phenotype (e.g., labs, imaging) is often more valuable than inferring risk from genotype alone. Functional medicine genetic panels (detox pathways, methylation SNPs, supplement protocols) lack clinical evidence and represent a common trap of accumulating data without actionable insight. The framework: match the test type to a specific clinical question, evaluate by effect size and actionability, and only test when results can genuinely influence a decision.
