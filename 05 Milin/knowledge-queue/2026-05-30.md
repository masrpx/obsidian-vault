## 1. Growth hormone for musculoskeletal system repair
- source: https://peterattiamd.com/growth-hormone-for-musculoskeletal-system-repair/
- type: rss
- vault_path: 03 Resources/Biohacking
- relevance: แม็กออกหนังและสนใจ biohacking อย่างจริงจัง—บทความนี้ช่วย debunk GH/secretagogue hype ที่มักถูกขายในคลินิกและวงการ longevity โดยตรง มีประโยชน์ทั้งในการตัดสินใจส่วนตัวเรื่อง performance/recovery และการให้คำแนะนำที่ถูกต้องแก่คนไข้คลินิกรัชตกายา
- approved: false

Human growth hormone (hGH) and GH secretagogues (tesamorelin, ibutamoren, ipamorelin) are widely marketed for tissue repair, anti-aging, and performance, but clinical evidence in non-deficient adults is strikingly thin. The mechanism is real—GH raises IGF-1, activates mTOR/AKT anabolic signaling, and stimulates collagen synthesis in preclinical models—but systemic hGH is a blunt intervention that also causes insulin resistance, fluid retention, and body-wide connective tissue changes rather than targeted local repair. Three distinct claims must be separated: (1) accelerating MSK tissue repair (weak evidence), (2) reducing lean-mass loss during immobilization/surgery (more plausible but narrow), and (3) anti-aging rejuvenation in healthy adults (mostly biological extrapolation without meaningful human outcomes). Legitimate hGH use is confined to documented deficiency, severe burns, or HIV wasting—applying it to orthopedic recovery or longevity optimization in healthy adults is a different and poorly supported category of medicine.

## 3. Resistance training: lowering the barrier to entry
- source: https://peterattiamd.com/lowering-the-barrier-to-entry-for-resistance-training/
- type: rss
- vault_path: 03 Resources/Biohacking
- relevance: แม็กออกหนังสม่ำเสมอและสนใจ Biohacking — งานวิจัยนี้ช่วย calibrate ว่าสำหรับคนรอบข้าง (ทีมคลินิก, ครอบครัว, หรือแม้แต่ตัวเองช่วง recovery) ไม่จำเป็นต้อง optimize ทุกอย่างเพื่อได้ผลลัพธ์ด้านสุขภาพที่ดี — relevant โดยเฉพาะเมื่อลูกแฝดเกิดกลางปี 2026 และเวลาจะจำกัดมากขึ้น
- approved: false

Meta-analysis of 192 studies shows untrained individuals can capture most strength benefits, near-maximal hypertrophy, and full mobility improvement with just moderate loads (<80% 1RM), multiple sets, and only 2 sessions per week — dubbed the LM2 protocol. This challenges the common 'optimize everything' approach (heavy loads, high frequency, near-failure) that creates high barriers to entry. The key insight is distinguishing between what is *optimal* vs. what is *sufficient* to obtain meaningful health benefits — particularly combating age-related sarcopenia, preserving amino acid reserves, and protecting metabolic function during illness or stress. For beginners or those restarting, a low-complexity, lower-frequency program is not a compromise — it's strategically appropriate for their training status.
