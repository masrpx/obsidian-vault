## 2. Growth hormone for musculoskeletal system repair
- source: https://peterattiamd.com/growth-hormone-for-musculoskeletal-system-repair/
- type: rss
- vault_path: 03 Resources/Biohacking
- relevance: Max's biohacking interest likely involves evaluating peptides/secretagogues for recovery and longevity optimization — this note provides a critical evidence-based framework to avoid overhyped GH protocols, especially relevant as physical performance and long-term health become priorities ahead of raising twin daughters in 2026.
- approved: false

hGH and GH secretagogues (tesamorelin, ibutamoren, ipamorelin) are widely promoted for tissue repair, anti-catabolism, and anti-aging, but clinical evidence in non-deficient adults is strikingly thin. The mechanistic rationale is real — GH raises IGF-1, activates mTOR/PI3K/AKT, and drives collagen synthesis in preclinical models — but systemic hGH is a blunt tool: it induces insulin resistance, fluid retention, and body-wide connective tissue changes rather than targeted local repair. The three core claims (accelerate tissue repair, blunt immobilization muscle loss, reverse age-related GH decline) must be evaluated separately and have very different evidence profiles — weakest for tissue repair, somewhat plausible but narrow for anti-catabolism, mostly extrapolation for anti-aging. Legitimate use remains confined to documented GH deficiency, severe burns, or HIV wasting; applying it to healthy adults post-orthopedic surgery or as a longevity intervention lacks convincing human outcome data (pain, function, strength, return to activity).

## 3. #393 ‒ AMA #85: A guide to medications and supplements: determining what to take, what to skip, and how to know if they’re working for you
- source: https://peterattiamd.com/ama85/
- type: rss
- vault_path: 03 Resources/Biohacking
- relevance: Max is actively biohacking and likely evaluates supplements/medications for performance and longevity; this framework directly upgrades his decision-making process with rigorous, falsifiable self-experimentation protocols aligned with his INTJ systematic thinking style.
- approved: false

Before choosing any medication or supplement, define the problem with a measurable metric, a specific target threshold, and a time horizon — vague goals like 'more energy' or 'better longevity' are unactionable and unfalsifiable. The standard of evidence required should scale with the intervention's purpose: the higher the stakes or irreversibility, the stronger the proof needed. Mechanistic reasoning alone is insufficient justification; relative risk statistics are misleading without absolute risk context and baseline risk data. Supplements deserve more skepticism than medications due to weak regulatory oversight, poor quality control, contamination risks, and marketing-driven overuse. Structure self-experiments with clear before/after measurement, periodic reevaluation, and willingness to discontinue — only a small subset of OTC supplements pass a reasonable evidence-to-downside-risk threshold.

Chung Ju-yung founded Hyundai and became one of history's most relentless builders, helping transform South Korea from a war-torn nation into an industrial powerhouse. At its peak, Hyundai contributed 16% of South Korea's entire GDP — spanning highways, shipbuilding, automobiles, and entire industries built from scratch. His story is driven by hunger, guilt, discipline, and an unwillingness to quit when no clear path existed. The core lesson is that scale is achieved through persistence and vertical integration of vision, not just capital or opportunity.
