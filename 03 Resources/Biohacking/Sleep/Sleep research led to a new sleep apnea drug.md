AD109 is a new oral drug for obstructive sleep apnea currently in Phase 3 trials, showing significant reduction in breathing interruptions and improved oxygen levels vs placebo. It targets two neural pathways identified by U of T researcher Richard Horner: the loss of noradrenaline 'go' signal and muscarinic receptor 'stop' signal that together collapse tongue muscle tone during REM sleep. Over 1.6 billion adults globally are affected, yet fewer than 10% are formally diagnosed. AD109 combines a noradrenaline booster and a muscarinic receptor blocker in a single daily pill — a pharmacological alternative to CPAP machines.
