Genetic testing is powerful but widely misunderstood — its value depends heavily on the clinical question being asked, not the test itself. Some findings (e.g., BRCA mutations, APOE for Alzheimer's, inherited cardiac conditions) are genuinely actionable and life-altering, while most consumer genetic tests offer probabilistic risk estimates with limited clinical utility. A key insight: directly measuring phenotype (biomarkers, scans, labs) is often more informative than inferring risk from DNA alone. Functional medicine genetic testing (methylation, detox pathways, supplement protocols) largely lacks clinical evidence despite biological plausibility. The ideal framework: match a clear clinical question → appropriate test type → lab with rigorous interpretation → decision that meaningfully changes behavior or treatment.
