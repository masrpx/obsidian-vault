Genetic testing is not a blueprint for predicting all future diseases — its value depends entirely on the clinical question being asked. Some tests are genuinely life-altering (e.g., BRCA for cancer risk, APOE for Alzheimer's, inherited cardiac conditions), while others like functional medicine panels (methylation, detox genes) lack clinical evidence despite biological plausibility. A key principle: directly measuring the phenotype is often more informative than inferring risk from genotype alone. Peter's framework evaluates tests by effect size and actionability — only pursue genetic testing when it's matched to a specific question, uses the appropriate test type, and can meaningfully change a decision or behavior.
